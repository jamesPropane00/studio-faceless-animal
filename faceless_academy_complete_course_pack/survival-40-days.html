Important: Survival instruction cannot replace certified first-aid training, local regulations, expert field instruction, or emergency services. Practice only in controlled conditions and never deliberately create a survival emergency.

1. Day 1–4: priorities and planning

Use a calm, evidence-based survival framework.

Core knowledge

  • Stop, assess hazards, treat immediate life threats, and make a plan.
  • Prioritize shelter, signaling, water, and food according to conditions.
  • Tell someone your route before leaving.
  • Avoid turning a manageable situation into an injury.

2. Day 5–8: first aid

Stabilize emergencies and seek professional rescue.

Core knowledge

  • Control severe bleeding with direct pressure and appropriate emergency methods.
  • Protect airway and breathing.
  • Treat suspected spinal injury cautiously.
  • Learn CPR and first aid through certified hands-on training.

3. Day 9–12: shelter

Reduce exposure to heat, cold, wind, and precipitation.

Core knowledge

  • Site selection matters more than elaborate construction.
  • Insulate from the ground.
  • Ventilate any enclosed shelter.
  • Never use unsafe flame or combustion in a confined space.

4. Day 13–16: water

Find, carry, and treat water.

Core knowledge

  • Clear water can still contain pathogens.
  • Boiling is a reliable biological treatment when performed correctly.
  • Filters vary in what they remove.
  • Chemical and UV treatment require correct dose, clarity, and contact conditions.

5. Day 17–20: fire

Use fire only when conditions and rules allow.

Core knowledge

  • Prepare tinder, kindling, and fuel before ignition.
  • Clear the area and control sparks.
  • Extinguish completely.
  • Wildfire restrictions override convenience.

6. Day 21–24: navigation

Avoid becoming more lost.

Core knowledge

  • Carry a map and compass and know how to use both.
  • Mark a last known point.
  • Terrain association supports navigation.
  • A phone GPS is useful but batteries and coverage fail.

7. Day 25–28: signaling

Make rescue easier.

Core knowledge

  • Three repeated signals commonly indicate distress.
  • Use contrast, movement, sound, and light appropriately.
  • A whistle conserves energy better than yelling.
  • Emergency beacons can transmit location without cellular service.

8. Day 29–32: food and sanitation

Avoid illness and poor decisions.

Core knowledge

  • Food is usually a lower immediate priority than exposure and water.
  • Do not eat unknown plants or mushrooms.
  • Store food away from sleeping areas where wildlife is a concern.
  • Hand and waste hygiene prevent disease.

9. Day 33–36: weather and hazards

Read conditions and manage risk.

Core knowledge

  • Check forecasts before travel.
  • Lightning, flash floods, heat, cold, wildlife, and terrain require different responses.
  • Turn around before conditions exceed skill and equipment.
  • Do not shelter under isolated tall objects during lightning.

10. Day 37–40: scenario drills

Combine planning, signaling, first aid, navigation, and shelter.

Core knowledge

  • Run tabletop scenarios before field practice.
  • Practice with a partner in controlled conditions.
  • Create vehicle, home, and hiking kits.
  • Debrief every drill and correct weaknesses.

Practice Exam

These are original exam-style questions, not copied test items.

1. You realize you are lost. What is the best first response?

Unplanned movement can increase search area and risk.

2. What is true of clear stream water?

Appearance does not establish microbiological safety.

3. Which signal conserves energy?

A whistle is louder per effort and can be repeated.

4. Where should you avoid placing a shelter?

Drainages and channels can flood rapidly.

5. What should you do with an unknown wild plant?

Misidentification can cause severe poisoning.

6. What is the safest fire policy?

Restrictions and complete extinguishment reduce wildfire and poisoning risk.
