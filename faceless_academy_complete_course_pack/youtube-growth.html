1. Viewer satisfaction

Design for the viewer, not for mythical algorithm tricks.

Core knowledge

  • Recommendations aim to help viewers find videos they want and support long-term satisfaction.
  • Personalization differs by viewer and context.
  • Videos are evaluated through performance and viewer response.
  • Different formats do not inherently confuse the system.

2. Packaging

Earn the click honestly.

Core knowledge

  • Title and thumbnail should make one clear promise.
  • Avoid packaging that attracts the wrong viewer.
  • Create curiosity without hiding the subject.
  • Compare click-through rate with retention and satisfaction.

3. Retention and structure

Deliver value after the click.

Core knowledge

  • Open quickly.
  • Create clear progression.
  • Use pattern changes where they support the story.
  • Cut repetition and dead time.

4. Search and discovery

Help viewers find useful answers.

Core knowledge

  • Use the actual query language naturally.
  • Create complete answers, not keyword stuffing.
  • Chapters and descriptions improve usability.
  • Related videos build topic depth.

5. Formats and analytics

Use Shorts, long-form, live, and posts strategically.

Core knowledge

  • Each piece is evaluated for interested viewers.
  • Study traffic sources separately.
  • Audience retention graphs reveal exits and replays.
  • Returning viewers indicate durable audience fit.

6. Monetization and policy

Build a channel that can survive review.

Core knowledge

  • Use original or meaningfully transformed content.
  • Disclose realistic altered or synthetic content where required.
  • Respect copyright and advertiser-friendly rules.
  • Do not buy fake engagement.

1. What does YouTube officially say recommendations prioritize?

Recommendations are personalized around viewer interest and satisfaction.

YouTube states experimenting with formats does not inherently confuse the algorithm.

3. What makes a strong title-thumbnail pair?

Packaging should attract the right viewer honestly.

Early exits often indicate an opening or expectation problem.

Durable growth comes from viewers repeatedly choosing and valuing content.
